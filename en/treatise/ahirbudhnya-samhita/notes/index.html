Notes on Readings and Translations

Ahirbudhnya-saṃhitā

1. Number of āsanas. The eleven principal poses are listed in verses 31–32 of the Ahirbudhnya-saṃhitā itself. Schrader also notes eleven principal āsanas in his survey of the chapter 6.

2. Verse numbering. Some verse numbers are omitted or not recognized by OCR in the supplied transcription. For Mayūrāsana, Wujastyk’s scholarly publication explicitly gives verses 36–37; for Siṃhāsana, 42–43; Muktāsana is divided into 44–45ab, and Gomukhāsana into 45cd–46 5. Thus verse 45 in the printed sequence falls across the boundary between two pose names.

3. The Kūrmāsana verse. The OCR of the supplied file incorrectly reads निपीय; the printed original has निपीड्य (nipīḍya). This is a reading checked against the scan, not a conjectural emendation.

4. Mayūrāsana, verses 36–37. The apparatus gives निरुध्य (D) in the first verse instead of अवष्टभ्य; E offers an alternative reading for the words describing the sides of the navel region. In verse 37, manuscripts E/F read मुच्यते instead of इष्यते, and B/C read निबर्हणम् instead of निवारणम्. The main printed readings are retained in the translation.

5. Vīrāsana, verse 39. The line पादमेकमैथेतरम् is left unemended; in the IAST and Cyrillic transliterations its place is marked as unclear. Its literal structure does not reliably establish the precise sequence of sides. Wujastyk’s comparison with the description in the Vimānārcanākalpa is cited only as an external scholarly parallel, not as an emendation to the text.

6. Bhadrāsana, verse 41. The reading निवेश्यापदे is retained as printed; अपदे cannot be interpreted with confidence here. The object of “having secured / bound with both hands” is also not stated explicitly.

7. Siṃhāsana, verses 42–43. Part of the word व्यात्तवक्त्रो is damaged or missing in the supplied transcription; the printed page permits it to be read as “with the mouth wide open.” This is a check against the printed original, not an addition from another text.

8. Muktāsana and Gomukhāsana, verses 44–46. The apparatus gives D’s variant च हस्तमध्ये for चाप्यङ्गमध्ये; E has another, not fully clear reading. In the next verse D reads प्रगृह्य for विगृह्य. The main reading is retained unchanged.

9. Verse 47. The form सानिभिश्च is unclear in the printed text. Only the grammatically discernible parts are translated; the meaning of the final word is not reconstructed.

10.Historical meanings of the names. The translations “lion pose,” “peacock pose,” and “cow-face pose” convey the meanings of the Sanskrit names but do not claim that these old descriptions are identical to modern āsanas bearing the same names. Scholarly literature notes substantial variation in descriptions of poses across texts and periods 5.
