The āsanas of the Dattātreyayogaśāstra

Dattātreyayogaśāstra · c. 1200–1250 CE · four paths of yoga · three āsanas

The System of Yoga in the Dattātreyayogaśāstra and its Postures

The Dattātreyayogaśāstra (c. 1200–1300 CE) is the earliest surviving text to teach physical techniques under the name Haṭhayoga. The text presents a comprehensive fourfold classification of yoga: 1. Mantrayoga (verses 12–14) — for the dull or weak practitioner (mṛdu / adhama). 2. Layayoga (verses 15–26) — the dissolution of the mind (cittalaya) through esoteric signs (saṃketa). 3. Haṭhayoga (verses 27–183) — the yoga of force, divided into two distinct historical lineages: - The Eightfold Yoga of Yājñavalkya (aṣṭāṅga, verses 28–130): identical in outline with Patañjali’s eight limbs, but integrating physical purification and prāṇāyāma. Under the third limb, āsana, the text notes that there are 8,400,000 poses, but teaches exclusively Padmāsana (verses 34–38). - The Mudrā system of Kapila and the Siddhas (verses 131–183): a system of physical mudrās designed to control and reverse the flow of vital fluids (bindu), breath, and internal heat. Here, the inverted posture Viparītakaraṇī is taught (verses 146–149). 4. Rājayoga (verses 184–193) — the royal yoga of samādhi and spiritual liberation, which is the ultimate fruition of the physical practices.

Thus, while post-15th-century anthologies like the Haṭhapradīpikā classify 15 postures under a single āsana chapter, the Dattātreyayogaśāstra describes three primary bodily postures, distributed across its distinct structural divisions: - Padmāsana (verses 34–38) — the solitary āsana of the eightfold path; - Śavāsana (verses 24cd–26) — the bodily dissolution technique of Layayoga; - Viparītakaraṇī (verses 146–149) — the bodily inversion technique classified among the Haṭhayoga mudrās.

All three descriptions served as the direct word-for-word textual prototypes borrowed by Svātmārāma in the Haṭhapradīpikā (HP 1.45–47, HP 1.32, and HP 3.79–82) and the Śivasaṃhitā (ŚS 3.102–105, ŚS 4.69–72).

This is how the verse is printed in the critical edition; below are the translation and every manuscript named in its apparatus, so that nothing has to be looked up in another section.

इदं पद्मासनं प्रोक्तं सर्व्याधिविनाशनम् ।दुर्लभं येन केनापि धीमता लभ्यते भुवि ॥

idaṃ padmāsanaṃ proktaṃ sarvavyādhivināśanam /durlabhaṃ yena kenāpi dhīmatā labhyate bhuvi //

Edition translation This is called the lotus pose [and] it cures all diseases. It is difficult for just anyone to accomplish; it is accomplished by a wise person [here] on earth.

Our rendering This lotus pose destroys all diseases; it is hard to attain and is won on earth only by the wise.
