Concordance and conclusions

Dattātreyayogaśāstra

Comparative Concordance: Dattātreyayogaśāstra and Haṭhapradīpikā

The table below demonstrates how Svātmārāma directly utilized the Dattātreyayogaśāstra as his primary textual source when drafting the Haṭhapradīpikā (around 1400–1450 CE):

Posture / TechniqueDattātreyayogaśāstra (around 1200 CE)Haṭhapradīpikā (Oxford edited by , 2024)Nature of Textual Adaptation
PadmāsanaVerses 34–38 (caturaśītilakṣeṣv… uttānau caraṇau… nāsāgre vinyased…)Verses 1.45–47 (uttānau caraṇau… nāsāgre vinyased… idaṃ padmāsanaṃ… )Direct Verbatim Borrowing: Svātmārāma lifted verses 35–38 almost word-for-word, integrating Dattātreya’s seated prāṇāyāma foundation as his second description of the lotus pose.
ŚavāsanaVerse 24cd (uttānaśavavad bhūmau śayanaṃ coktam uttamam)Verse 1.32 (uttānaśavavad bhūmau śayanaṃ tu śavāsanam / sarvāsanaśrānti …)Structural Elevation: Dattātreya’s Layayoga saṃketa for mental dissolution (cittalaya) was converted into a named physical āsana, and augmented with a second line on relieving fatigue.
ViparītakaraṇīVerses 146–149 (karaṇaṃ viparītākhyaṃ… āhāro bahulas tasya saṃpādyaḥ sāṃkṛte…)Verses 3.79–82 (karaṇaṃ viparītākhyaṃ… āhāro bahulo grāhyaḥ sādhakasya…)Editorial Adaptation: Svātmārāma borrowed the 4-verse description of the inversion, removing the personal address to disciple Sāṃkṛti and slightly refining the compound jaṭharāgni-vivardhanam.

Textological Summary of Manuscript Transmission

  1. The Southern Recension (M1, M2, A, P, T): Transmitted primarily in Grantha characters on palm-leaf folios across Tamil Nadu and Karnataka. These witnesses preserve an older textual stratum. In verse 34, they read śivena kathitaṃ yat tu instead of ādināthena, and consistently maintain the full vocative addresses to Sāṃkṛti.
  2. The Northern & Western Recension (Jodhpur, Baroda, Vārāṇasī, Awasthi): Transmitted on paper in Devanāgarī. These witnesses introduced standardization towards standard classical Sanskrit forms (such as adjusting the sandhi kṛtvorusaṃsthau in Awasthi and Baroda). They share close textual affinities with the manuscripts consulted by the 17th-century commentator Śivānandasarasvatī for his Yogacintāmaṇi.
  3. Omission of intermediate gymnastic poses: The absence of the 11 non-seated āsanas (such as Mayūrāsana, Kukkuṭāsana, Dhanurāsana) in the Dattātreyayogaśāstra is not an accidental loss or manuscript defect; it proves that in the 13th-century Deccan, physical yoga was structured around seated meditation postures for prāṇāyāma (Padmāsana), somatic relaxation for dissolution (Śavāsana), and internal fluid-reversal mudrās (Viparītakaraṇī). The synthesis with external gymnastic poses was a later development of the 14th-century Śārṅgadharapaddhati and 15th-century Haṭhapradīpikā.
