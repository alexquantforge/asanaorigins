How to read the apparatus (symbols)

Haṭhapradīpikā

  • “In the edition — …” — so this passage is printed in the critical edition; after that the variants found in the manuscripts are listed, and the manuscripts themselves are named.
  • “Also “…” — so in: …” — the manuscripts listed after the colon have this reading. “So in: …” after the words “In the edition” — precisely these manuscripts support the edition’s reading (not all the rest).
  • In all the other manuscripts — the edition’s formula (Latin ceteri): all the manuscripts except those listed support the edition’s reading; when it says “including Government Oriental Manuscripts Library, manuscript 18885, Chennai”, that manuscript is among them.
  • Conjecture of the editors — a reading found in no manuscript: the editors restored it themselves.
  • ° (a small circle) — a sign of the edition’s apparatus: the quotation is given in abbreviated form (not from the beginning or not to the end of the word). There are six such places in the text — verses 1.24, 1.35, 1.42, 1.44 and twice 1.56 — and all of them are expanded here to their full form: instead of “°m aṅghrimūla” the document reads “…yonisthānakam aṅghrimūla…”, and next to it the passage is quoted as printed in the edition’s apparatus. In the editors’ comments ° marks a word boundary and has been replaced with a hyphen, for example “-doṣṇā”, “tatkūrpara-”.
  • <<…>> (double angle brackets) — so the letters are written in the manuscript itself (traces of a correction or an interlinear addition).
  • “before / after correction by the scribe” — the passage was corrected by the scribe himself; the edition records both states.
  • a, b, c, d — the quarters (pādas) of a verse: a — first, b — second, c — third, d — fourth. “1.53ab” means “the first two quarters of verse 1.53”.
  • Word-by-word analysis — every word of the verse separately, with its meaning; sandhi is resolved, compounds are split with hyphens, and variant readings are noted in brackets where needed.
  • Literal translation — a rendering “word by word”, following the order of the verse; Literary translation — the same sense in flowing English.
  • Every section has the same blocks: the apparatus verse by verse, and at the end of the section the word-by-word analysis, the two translations (literal and literary) and the editors’ commentary.
  • Edition translation (English) — the translation printed in the critical edition itself; Our rendering — our own translation, given just below for comparison. Both are in English; where they differ, the difference is meaningful.
  • + (plus) in a manuscript reading — letters illegible or lost (for example “eka + + +” means that in the manuscript something unclear is read in place of these syllables).
  • A manuscript is given in full the first time it is named inside a verse (collection, shelfmark, city; the expansions of Nepalese-German Manuscript Preservation Project, microfilm and Government Oriental Manuscripts Library, manuscript stand right there); further mentions of the same manuscript within that verse are shortened, for example “Nepalese-German Manuscript Preservation Project, microfilm B 62-20, Nepal”, “Government Oriental Manuscripts Library, manuscript 18885, Chennai”.
  • Leaves are cited as in the edition: “folio 84”, “folio 84, verso” (verso).

Editors’ commentary at the end of each section is given as in the edition (in English), with the sigla replaced by shelfmarks and the abbreviations expanded.
