Postures in the Matsyendrasaṃhitā: Chapter Three

Author: Alexander Vijaya (Telegram)

Tantric yoga · edited by Debabrata Sensharma · Calcutta: The Asiatic Society, 1994 · Bibliotheca Indica, number 318 · chapter 3 on Sanskrit text pages 21–25

Preface

The Matsyendrasaṃhitā is a Sanskrit tantric treatise on yoga attributed to the siddha Matsyendranātha. Its third paṭala, or chapter, describes fifteen bodily postures, or āsanas. The following presentation includes the Sanskrit text, transliteration in IAST and Cyrillic, word-by-word glosses, literal and literary translations, and notes on difficult passages.

The Sanskrit text is based on Debabrata Sensharma’s 1994 edition. Its parentheses, question marks and ellipses are retained. In the translations, square brackets mark explanatory additions or proposed interpretations. An uncertain interpretation is not necessarily an illegible printed word: a form can be clearly printed and still be grammatically difficult.

The work attributes the removal of disease and the attainment of siddhis—perfections or extraordinary powers—to these practices. These are claims made by the historical text, not medical recommendations.

The edition and its manuscript sources

Matsyendrasaṃhitā, ascribed to Matsyendranātha. Part I. Edited by Debabrata Sensharma. Calcutta: The Asiatic Society, 1994. Bibliotheca Indica, number 318. Digitised edition. Part I contains chapters 1–20 of 55. Chapter 3 appears on Sanskrit text pages 21–25.

The edition is based on a single manuscript known to the editor, held at the Wellcome Institute in London under the shelfmark 2/9/9. According to his description, it is written in Devanāgarī on locally made paper and consists of 120 folios, averaging 22 lines each. Sensharma notes lacunae and omissions and explains that he relied on emendation while leaving some passages in a corrupt form. Three incomplete manuscripts of the same title from the National Archives in Kathmandu, he reports, contained a different work and did not serve as witnesses to this text. Separately from the 1994 edition, P. C. Bagchi published four tantric works attributed to Matsyendranātha.

The readings and marks in a printed edition are not automatically identical to the manuscript: the edition also reflects editorial decisions. See Enrica Garzilli’s review for a discussion of the edition.

Text and numbering

Cyrillic transliteration follows a consistent mapping in which anusvāra is written м̣, with a dot below, and no soft sign is used. IAST represents the Devanāgarī text in Latin characters.

The verses are numbered sequentially here. In the printed edition, the verses numbered here as 9, 19, 29 and 39 bear the numbers ६, १६, २६ and ३६ respectively. A partially repeated verse between 17 and 18 is numbered 18a in the book. The labels [9a]–[10b] in the edition refer to source folios.

The postures are Vīrāsana, Kūrmāsana, Sudṛḍhāsana, Śvalikāsana (possibly Svastikāsana), Kukkuṭāsana, Mayūrāsana, Vyāghrāsana, Padmāsana, Vijayāsana, Dṛḍhāsana, Gṛdhrāsana, Garuḍāsana, Yakṣāsana, Siddhāsana and Vṛṣabhāsana. The chapter closes with general instructions on practice.
