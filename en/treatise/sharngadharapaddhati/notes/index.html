Manuscripts and Editions

Śārṅgadharapaddhati

This section was written for the site: it is not in the source PDFs and is not counted by the coverage check. The list of manuscripts A–F comes from Peterson’s preface of 1888, the other shelfmarks from Aufrecht’s catalogue and from library descriptions; the links are given at the end. The text of the treatise itself stays verbatim, in the other sections.

Printed editions

There is no complete critical edition, and no complete translation into any language. Both PDF volumes rest on Peterson’s edition of 1888 — the only complete printed edition of the Sanskrit text.

Not to be confused: the Bṛhat-Śārṅgadharapaddhati (18th c., 588 sections) is an expanded compendium, and the Śārṅgadhara-saṃhitā is a medical treatise, possibly by another author.

The manuscripts Peterson used

Six copies described in the preface to volume I (1888); the letters A–F belong to the edition.

  • A — incomplete, bought for the Government of Bombay in 1886–7, leaves 1–253, breaking off in the middle of verse 4153. Undated: from its appearance Peterson places it in the 16th century. In his judgement, the most correct copy.
  • B — complete, also bought in 1886–7, leaves 1–295, down to verse 4620, the proper end. Very beautifully written by a Jain scribe, but neither so old nor so correct as A.
  • C — the copy of Paṇḍit Durgāprasāda, Saṃvat 1776, complete; it contains the Khaḍgaparīkṣā chapter, which Peterson placed at the end of the book as an appendix.
  • D — incomplete, lent by Jyeṣṭārāma Mukundjī, leaves 1–144, breaking off in the middle of verse 1588.
  • E — lent through the same bookseller by Seth Dhanay-Mahīuvāyī, Saṃvat 1819, complete (the owner’s name is hard to read in the reprint).
  • F — a copy from the Palace Library at Jodhpur, Saṃvat 1877; used for the last part of the book, where A was wanting.

  • London, Royal Asiatic Society — Tod 32: paper, Devanāgarī; given to the Society by James Tod on 21 February 1824. The Society’s description shows fol. 282r with the words «atha yogaḥ». Description

  • Delhi, IGNCA, VMR 688: a microfilm of a manuscript from Pune (Vaidika Saṃśodhana Maṇḍala), 289 ff. Catalogue record

What remains unknown

  • The sigla of Aufrecht’s catalogue are given as they stand in the catalogue: they are not expanded into modern shelfmarks here, and some collections have moved since (India Office → British Library).
  • The letters E and F are hard to read in the 1987 reprint: the descriptions of the copies are reliable, the letters themselves are given with that caveat.
  • The dates of A and B are not from colophons but Peterson’s estimate from the paper; he says himself that they «can only be conjectured».
  • Tod 32 is dated differently in different places: the English PDF calls it a late copy, the Royal Asiatic Society’s description suggests the 17th century.
  • There is still no complete critical edition and no complete translation — a commonplace in the literature on the text.
