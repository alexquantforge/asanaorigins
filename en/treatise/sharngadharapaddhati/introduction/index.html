PART II. THE COMPLETE CORPUS OF ĀSANAS IN THE ŚĀRṄGADHARAPADDHATI

Śārṅgadharapaddhati · chapters 157–158 · the haṭha-yoga section · nine verses on āsanas

The Haṭhayoga sections of the Śārṅgadharapaddhati are systematically divided into two distinct historical chapters: 1. Chapter 157 (verses 4372–4419): The Six-Limbed Haṭhayoga Well-Practiced by Gorakṣa and Others (Gorakṣādisusādhitaḥ prathamo haṭhayogaḥ); 2. Chapter 158 (verses 4420–4448): The Eight-Limbed Haṭhayoga Practiced by Mārkaṇḍeya and Others (Mārkaṇḍeyādisādhito dvitīyo haṭhayogaḥ).

SECTION A. THE SIX-LIMBED YOGA OF GORAKṢA (CHAPTER 157)

1. Verse 4375: Preparation of the Seat (Āsanavidhi)

4375

एकान्ते विजने देशे पवित्रे निरुपद्रवे ।कम्बलाजिनवस्त्राणामु पर्य्यासनमभ्यसेत् ॥ ४३७५ ॥

ekānte vijane deśe pavitre nirupadrave /kambalājinavastrāṇām upary āsanam abhyaset // 4375 //

Metre: Anuṣṭubh

Translation

“In a solitary, secluded, pure, and safe place, one should practice the posture upon a seat composed of a woolen blanket, an antelope skin, and cloth.”

SECTION B. THE EIGHT-LIMBED YOGA OF MĀRKAṆḌEYA (CHAPTER 158)

4. Verses 4428–4430: General Rules of Practice and Seat Construction

4428–4430

nādhyātaḥ kṣudhitaḥ śrānto na ca vyākulacetanaḥ /yuñjīta yogaṃ yogajño nityaṃ siddhy-artham ādṛtaḥ // 4428 //na śīte nāti caivoṣṇe na durdhe nāmbu-saṃnidhau /na sopadrava-deśeṣu yogaṃ saṃsādhayet kvacit // 4429 //ekānte vijane ramye pavitre nirupadrave /sukhāsane samādhiḥ syāt kuśājinacelottare // 4430 //

Metre: Anuṣṭubh

Translation

“The knower of yoga should never practice when agitated by worries, hungry, exhausted, or with a perturbed mind; for the sake of success, he should engage in practice with diligent care (4428). Neither in intense cold nor extreme heat, neither in bad weather nor near rushing waters, nor in perilous places should one ever practice yoga (4429). In a solitary, secluded, pleasant, pure, and safe place, absorption (samādhi) occurs on an easy seat arranged in layers of kuśa grass, an antelope skin, and a cloth on top (4430).”

5. Verse 4431: Enunciation of the Three Primary Postures

4431

पद्ममर्धासनं चापि तथा सिद्धासनादिकम् ।आस्थाय योगं युञ्जीत कृत्वा च प्रणवं हृदि ॥ ४४३१ ॥

padmam ardhāsanaṃ cāpi tathā siddhāsanādikam /āsthāya yogaṃ yuñjīta kṛtvā ca praṇavaṃ hṛdi // 4431 //

Metre: Anuṣṭubh

Peterson’s edition contains a typographical error: padmam adhāsanaṃ. The emendation ardhāsanaṃ (“half-lotus”) is certain from the description in verse 4436.

6. Verses 4432–4434: Biomechanics of the Seated Posture

4432–4434

samaḥ samāsano bhūtvā saṃhṛtya caraṇāv ubhau /saṃvṛtāsyaḥ tathā coraḥ samyag viṣṭabhya cāgrataḥ // 4432 //pārṣṇibhyāṃ liṅga-vṛṣaṇāv aspṛśann agrayataḥ sthitaḥ /kiñcid unnamitaś cirā dantair dantān na saṃspṛśet // 4433 //saṃpaśyen nāsikāgraṃ svaṃ diśaś cānavalokayan /kuryād dṛḍhaṃ pṛṣṭhavaṃśaṃ coḍḍīyānaṃ tathodare // 4434 //

Metre: Anuṣṭubh

Translation

“Becoming well-aligned on an even seat, drawing in both feet, keeping the mouth closed and thrusting the chest well forward (4432); positioned so as not to compress the genitals with the heels; slightly elevating the chin without clenching the teeth (4433); gazing at the tip of one’s own nose without glancing in other directions, one should keep the spine firm and erect (dṛḍhaṃ pṛṣṭhavaṃśam), and perform uḍḍīyāna in the abdomen (4434).”
