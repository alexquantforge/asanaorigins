1.2. The Chronological Problem: Scholarly Arguments and Dating Debate

The historical dating of the Haṭhatattvakaumudī has been examined by leading Sanskritists and historians of yoga:

1. Arguments of Dr. M. L. Gharote and Dr. M. Venkata Reddy (Critical Edition, 2007):

  • Terminus post quem (Earliest possible date): Sundaradeva directly cites late seventeenth-century works:
  • «Haṭharatnāvalī» (Haṭharatnāvalī) by Śrīnivāsa (Śrīnivāsa), composed circa 1670–1695 CE. Sundaradeva adopts its specialized terminology, āsana lists, and quotes its verses;
  • «Kumbhakapaddhati» (Kumbhakapaddhati) by Raghuvīra (Raghuvīra), datable to the late seventeenth or early eighteenth century;
  • «Yogacandrikā» (Yogacandrikā) and «Yogacintāmaṇi» (Yogacintāmaṇi) by Śivānanda Sarasvatī.
  • Terminus ante quem (Latest possible date): The oldest and only complete dated manuscript witness (MS. No. 4235, Scindia Oriental Institute, Ujjain) preserves a scribal colophon with the exact date of completion: Saṃvat 1846 (Vikrama Saṃvat), month of Vaiśākha, dark fortnight, 10th tithi, Tuesday, corresponding to April–May 1789 CE.
  • Gharote's Conclusion: The treatise was composed between 1700 and 1750 CE (first half of the eighteenth century).

2. Textological Arguments of Dr. Jason Birch (Oxford University / Haṭha Yoga Project):

  • Dr. Jason Birch points out that the Haṭhatattvakaumudī epitomizes the late medieval "encyclopedic turn" in yoga. This genre of comprehensive compendia, synthesizing hundreds of earlier sources (from Upaniṣads and Tantras to late medieval paddhatis), flourished among the scholarly Brahmin milieus of Vārāṇasī precisely in the first half of the eighteenth century.
  • Birch underscores that Sundaradeva's Sanskrit exhibits late classical syntax infused with the scientific medical lexicon of early modern India.

3. Iconographic and Structural Analysis of Prof. Gudrun Bühnemann:

  • Prof. Bühnemann examined the evolution of āsana classification schemes in Indian traditions. In Chapter 7 of the Haṭhatattvakaumudī, Sundaradeva retains the canonical core of 15 postures (inherited from Chapter 1 of Svātmārāma's Haṭhapradīpikā), yet reinterprets them within a therapeutic methodology and introduces distinctive technical nuances (e.g., yonyāsana as an advanced modification of siddhāsana, specialized breath management during padmāsana). This marks a systematic transition before the proliferation of vast catalogs of 84 or more āsanas in late eighteenth- and nineteenth-century manuals.

4. Sociological Synthesis by Dr. Mark Singleton:

  • Mark Singleton emphasizes that Sundaradeva represents a notable synthesis of two social roles: the practicing yoga adept and the high-caste, university-educated vaidya-paṇḍita. Such integration was characteristic of cosmopolitan Vārāṇasī in the eighteenth century, where Haṭha yoga techniques were absorbed into the orthodox curriculum of health, longevity, and therapeutics.

वामोरुमूले दक्षिणपादयुग्मं¹ निवेशयेद् वामपदेन युक्तम्² ।पृष्ठं समावेष्ट्य कृताङ्गसंस्थः प्रचण्डमत्स्येन्द्रमतं प्रसिद्धम्³ ॥ ७ ॥

vāmoru-mūle dakṣiṇapāda-yugmaṃ¹ niveśayed vāmapadena yuktam² ।pṛṣṭhaṃ samāveṣṭya kṛtāṅgasaṃsthaḥ pracaṇḍa-matsyendra-mataṃ prasiddham³ ॥ 7 ॥

Word-by-Word Grammatical Analysis (Verse 7)

vāmoru-mūle [vāma-ūru-mūla-]
noun, neuter, locative singular (compound: vāma ["left"] + ūru ["thigh"] + mūla ["root, base"]) — "at the root of the left thigh";
dakṣiṇapāda-yugmam [dakṣiṇa-pāda-yugma-]
noun, neuter, accusative singular — "the right foot";
niveśayet [ni-viś]
verb, optative causative, 3rd person singular — "one should place / let him position";
vāmapadena [vāma-pada-]
noun, masculine, instrumental singular — "with the left foot";
yuktam [yukta-]
past passive participle of root yuj, neuter, accusative singular (agreeing with pādayugmam) — "joined, placed alongside";
pṛṣṭham [pṛṣṭha-]
noun, neuter, accusative singular — "the back / torso";
samāveṣṭya [sam-ā-veṣṭ]
absolutive from root veṣṭ ["to twist, wrap"] — "having twisted, rotating the torso";
kṛtāṅgasaṃsthaḥ [kṛta-aṅga-saṃstha-]
bahuvrīhi adjective, masculine, nominative singular — "having stabilized the posture of the body";
pracaṇḍa-matsyendra-matam [pracaṇḍa-matsyendra-mata-]
noun, neuter, nominative/accusative singular — "the doctrine of the formidable Matsyendra";
prasiddham [prasiddha-]
past passive participle / adjective, neuter, nominative singular — "renowned, celebrated".

मत्स्येन्द्रपीठं जठरप्रदीप्तिं प्रचण्डरुङ्मण्डलखण्डनास्त्रम्⁴ ।अभ्यासतः कुण्डलिनीप्रबोधं चन्द्रस्थिरं पुंसि ददाति नूनम् ॥ ८ ॥

matsyendrapīṭhaṃ jaṭharapradīptiṃ pracaṇḍa-ruṅ-maṇḍala-khaṇḍanāstram⁴ ।abhyāsataḥ kuṇḍalinī-prabodhaṃ candrasthiraṃ puṃsi dadāti nūnam ॥ 8 ॥

Word-by-Word Grammatical Analysis (Verse 8)

matsyendrapīṭham [matsyendra-pīṭha-]
noun, neuter, nominative singular — "the seat of Matsyendra (matsyendrapīṭha)";
jaṭharapradīptim [jaṭhara-pradīpti-]
noun, feminine, accusative singular (compound: jaṭhara ["belly, stomach"] + pradīpti ["kindling, blazing"]) — "the kindling of the gastric fire";
pracaṇḍa-ruṅ-maṇḍala-khaṇḍanāstram [pracaṇḍa-ruj-maṇḍala-khaṇḍana-astra-]
compound adjective, neuter, nominative singular (compound: pracaṇḍa ["violent, fierce"] + ruj ["disease, affliction"; sandhi: -j to -ṅ- before nasal] + maṇḍala ["multitude, circle"] + khaṇḍana ["shattering, destroying"] + astra ["weapon, missile"]) — "serving as a destructive weapon against the host of fierce diseases";
abhyāsataḥ [abhyāsa-]
noun with ablative suffix -tas — "through regular practice";
kuṇḍalinī-prabodham [kuṇḍalinī-prabodha-]
noun, masculine, accusative singular — "the awakening of Kuṇḍalinī";
candrasthiram [candra-sthira-]
noun, neuter, accusative singular — "the stabilization of the nectar of the moon (amṛta)";
puṃsi [puṃs-]
noun, masculine, locative singular — "in a man / in the practitioner";
dadāti [dā]
verb, present active 3rd class, 3rd person singular — "it bestows, grants";
nūnam
indeclinable emphatic particle — "surely, verily, without doubt".

Literal Translation (Verses 7–8)

At the root of the left thigh let him place the right foot joined with the left foot; having twisted the back, having stabilized the body, [this is] the renowned doctrine of formidable Matsyendra. Matsyendra's seat, being a weapon for shattering the multitude of fierce diseases, through practice verily bestows upon a man the kindling of the gastric fire, the awakening of Kuṇḍalinī, and the steadiness of the moon.

Literary Translation (Verses 7–8)

Placing the right foot at the base of the left thigh and positioning the left foot on the outside, the yogin rotates his spine and locks his body in a firm twist. This is the celebrated teaching of the mighty master Matsyendranātha. Matsyendra's seat (matsyendrapīṭha) acts as a shattering weapon against the host of virulent diseases. Through regular practice, it unfailingly fans the digestive fire of the belly, awakens the dormant Kuṇḍalinī, and eternally arrests the downward dissipation of the lunar nectar.

Textological Commentary and Critical Apparatus

  • Footnote 1 to Verse 7: Witness «b» interpolates the particle 'tha (atha), breaking the smooth rhythm of the Upajāti meter preserved in «a».
  • Footnote 2 to Verse 7: In «a», the neuter participle yuktam agrees with pādayugmam; in «b», the masculine yuktaḥ shifts agreement to the practitioner.
  • Footnote 4 to Verse 8: In «a», khaṇḍanāstram (-astram — "weapon of destruction"); in «b», khaṇḍanāntam (-antam — "terminating in destruction"). The martial metaphor of astra is typical of tantric yoga; reading «b» is a graphic scribal misreading of stra as nta.
