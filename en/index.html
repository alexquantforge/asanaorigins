Mataṅgapārameśvara(āgama), Yogapāda

Śaiva Siddhānta · upāgama

  • ParyaṅkaMataṅgapārameśvara, Yogapāda 2.13; 2.15–17b
  • KamalaMataṅgapārameśvara, Yogapāda 2.13; 2.17c–18
  • BhadraMataṅgapārameśvara, Yogapāda 2.13; 2.19c–20b
  • SvastikaMataṅgapārameśvara, Yogapāda 2.13; 2.20c–22b

Parākhya(tantra)

Postures: Parākhya 14.5 — padma, svastika, daṇḍa, ardhacandra; the definition of ardhacandra in 14.6c–7b (per Kafle). See Part IV, section 11.3.

Vijñānabhairava

Bodily instructions. Some methods describe a body position: sit on a soft seat on one buttock without support for arms and legs (78); sit with arms slightly bent (79); on a rocking seat (83) (Part IV, section 1.11).

Uḍḍāmareśvaratantra

Postures: ekapādasthita (“standing on one leg”) during japa, 12.30 (Part IV, section 1.12).

Toḍalatantra

Postures: yonimudrāsana (2.17–20): with the body straight, bring the nose to the knees, grasping the knees with the hands, together with breath retention (Part IV, section 1.12).

Vīṇāśikhatantra

Postures: padmāsanopaviṣṭa in the description of the deity, verse 96 (Part IV, section 1.12).

Tantrāloka of Abhinavagupta with Jayaratha’s commentary (viveka)

Postures: in chapters 15–38 (M00093) mentions of postures in ritual and yoga, Part IV, section 4.2.

Siddhayogeśvarīmata

Verse 16.47 places the vīra posture at japa. Verse 22.10 is iconography of the yoginīs; paryaṅkāsana is the editor’s emendation of the manuscripts’ «°āgata». Törzsök interprets paryaṅka as the ascetics’ seated posture, identical with vīrāsana; this is the editor’s interpretation, not the text. Both passages were checked against the edition (pages 40 and 51; Part IV, section 14).

Tantrasadbhāva

Postures: none in the published chapters (Part IV, section 14).

Jayadrathayāmala, ṣaṭka 4

Postures: phetkārīmudrā (4.2.612–622): sit down, place the left foot at the anus, stretch out the right leg, slightly bent. This passage exists for me only in Mallinson and Singleton’s translation; the Sanskrit I do not have (RootsofYoga 6.1.2; Part IV, section 10).

Brahmayāmala (French Institute of Pondicherry T522; M00319) — a late “pratiṣṭhā-tantra of the Mothers”

The catalogue summarizes line 994 as padmāsana with añjali-mudrā; the quoted line specifies the añjali-puṭa mudrā, after japa and bathing (Brahmayāmala: Muktabodha e-text number M00319, IFP transcript T00522, file line 994; Part IV, section 3.6). Separately, ālīḍha at line 3811 is included in Part IV, section 5, the iconography section on combat stances.

Piṅgalāmata (Jayadrathādhikāra; Pratiṣṭhākalpa)

Postures: iconographic ones — padmāsana, vīrāsana, yogāsana, ekapāda, the Buddha in padmāsana (Part IV, section 5).

Kaulajñānanirṇaya (Mahākaulajñānavinirṇaya)

Posture: ekapāda in visualization (10.1). Separately, 14.21 names the marks “tremor, leap.” See Part IV, section 5 and section 14.2.

Parātantra

Postures: ālīḍha, pratyālīḍha, yogapaṭṭa in the iconography (verses 59, 88, 104, 116). See Part IV, section 5.

Kaularahasya (Sarvāgamasāra)

Iconography: ālīḍha, a standing combat stance (Kaularahasya: Muktabodha e-text number M00326, Nepalese-German Manuscript Cataloguing Project manuscript 1-257, reel A-146/8, file line 2509; Part IV, section 5).

Amṛteśatantra (the Netratantra manuscript of 1101)

Postures: see Part IV, section 1.4 and section 5 (the Buddha in padmāsana).

Yoginīhṛdaya (with Amṛtānanda’s Dīpikā)

Postures: kāmeśvarāṅkaparyaṅka (1.52) — a “couch”, not a posture. Seats in the Śrīcakra — sādhyasiddhāsana and others. See Part IV, section 5 and section 7.

Kālottara (the South Indian version in 68 paṭalas; Government Oriental Manuscripts Library, Madras/Chennai; M00248)

Postures: chapter 25.7–17 — 20 names of postures “in this tantra” and karaṇa; 63.7–9 — svastika/padmāsana with body position. See Part IV, section 2.1.

Acintyaviśvasādākhya (Cintyaviśvasādākhya)

Postures: chapter 28.72–103 — 20 postures with descriptions (one of the most detailed passages in the āgamas); karaṇa; svastika; vīrāsana while eating, daṇḍāsana in the vow. See Part IV, section 2.2.

Suprabheda(āgama)

Postures: Yogapāda 3.57–63 — six postures with descriptions; Caryāpāda 6.39 — vīrāsana as a feat. See Part IV, section 2.3.

Makuṭāgama (M00036) / Mukuṭāgama (M00424)

Postures: a list of six names — gomukha, svastika, padma, ardhacandra, vīra, yogāsana — without descriptions. Besides, 2-167 baddhayogāsana and 2-111 kūrmāsana (here it is a seat, a piece of equipment). See Part IV, section 2.4.

Jñānasiddhi(yāgama) (E0006)

Postures: in the chapter on the eight-limbed yoga, verses 13–14, — a list of names: padma, gomukha, siṃha, svasti[ka], vīra, sukhāsana, mayūra, daṇḍāsana and others. See Part IV, section 2.5.

Kāmika (pūrvabhāga M00124 / uttarabhāga M00125)

Postures: pūrva 6.39 — “having assumed the desired posture, with a straight body, facing north” (at the pañcaśuddhi); in the iconography of the uttarabhāga — baddhapadmāsana, utkuṭāsana, vīrāsana (Dakṣiṇāmūrti); uttara 23.209cd–211a — karaṇa. See Part IV, section 2.6.

Aṃśumat (Aṃśumatkāśyapa, Aṃśumadāgama)

Postures: iconographic ones — utkuṭikāsana (Muktabodha M00198, file line 6286), the chapter on sukhāsanamūrti. The word padmāsana here also denotes an architectural element — a lotus base. See Part IV, section 2.6.

Mohacūḍottara

Postures: the iconographic ardhavīrāsana (Mohacūḍottara: Muktabodha electronic text number M00304, data-entered from the manuscript: Nepal-German Manuscript Cataloguing Project 5-1977, microfilm A 182/2; line number in that file: 542), see Part IV, section 2.6.

Yogaja(āgama)

Postures: Yogaja 25 — “having levelled the ground, sitting comfortably, facing north” (Yogajāgama: Muktabodha electronic text number M00250, data-entered from the manuscript: transcript of the French Institute of Pondicherry / French School of the Far East T0024; line number in that file: 724), see Part IV, section 2.6.

Sūkṣma(āgama)

Chapter 19 gives a ritual body-position instruction—only “with a straight body, silently”; the cited passage gives no posture name (Sūkṣmāgama: Muktabodha e-text number M00329, IFP/École française d’Extrême-Orient transcript T01003 copied from RE10779 of I.F.I., file line 102; Part IV, section 2.6).

Candrajñāna(āgama), Vīraśaiva rescension

Postures: only a prohibition: “prasārya na japet pādau kukkuṭāsana eva vā / yānaśayyādhirūḍho vā cintāvyākulito’pi vā” (translation: “One should not perform japa with legs stretched out, or else in the kukkuṭāsana, or mounted on a vehicle or bed, or being agitated by thoughts”). This is in the chapter on the five-syllable mantra (chapter 8, verse 81; Candrajñānāgama vīraśaiva rescension: Muktabodha electronic text number M00201, data-entered from the edition: jaṃgamavāḍī maṭha, ‹Vārāṇasī›; line numbers in that file: 1995–1996). A similar verse is in the Acintyaviśvasādākhya. See Part IV, section 2.2 and section 6.

Sakalāgamasārasaṅgraha

Postures: baddhavīrāsana while eating (Muktabodha M00063, file line 5224), see Part IV, section 2.6.

Śivadharma (the South Indian version; M00209)

Postures: none. There is only the yogapaṭṭa as a gift to a Śaiva yogin (Muktabodha M00209, file line 4359), see Part IV, section 2.6.

Kulārṇavatantra

Postures and instructions: 6.12 sukhāsana; 9.30 says yoga is not attained by means of padmāsana; 11.13 prohibits vīrāsana. The catalogue characterizes these passages mainly as prohibitions and general indications (Part IV, section 3.1).

Tantrarājatantra with the Manoramā commentary

Verse 5.20 concerns padmāsana; 27.59–63 gives postures in the chapter on breath (śvāsarūpa), where the commentary counts eight forms. See Part IV, section 3.2.

Śāradātilakatantra with Rāghavabhaṭṭa’s Padārthādarśa commentary

Verse 23.131 gives postures for ṣaṭkarman with definitions; 25.9 and 25.14–15 concern postures in the yoga chapter. See Part IV, section 3.3.

Gandharvatantra

Verses 6.5 and 6.45–56 give five postures; the editors’ note on 6.57 is also cited. See Part IV, section 3.3a.

Viśvasāratantra

The posture material is in untitled chapter 2 of the Muktabodha e-text M00101 (file lines 1561–1580). The catalogue links its block of five postures to Śāradātilaka 25, chapter 2, and separately notes svastika (M00101, file line 1664; Part IV, section 3.3).

Sarvollāsa(tantra)

Ullāsa 15.8–13 contains a block of five postures identified as a compilation. Ullāsa 20 quotes the Bhāvacūḍāmaṇi: in the absence of a corpse, vīrāsana is a viṣṭara, a seat or mat (M00120, file line 2776). See Part IV, sections 3.3 and 3.6.

Kaulāvalīnirṇaya

Verses 2.76–78 give an āsanavidhi in pūjā; 19.32–39 give five postures and kukkuṭa in the chapter on the six acts; 21.5 mentions baddhapadmāsana. See Part IV, sections 3.3 and 3.6.

Phetkāriṇītantra

Passages 3/180–182 give postures for the six acts, including ardhasvastika. See Part IV, section 3.4.

Bṛhannīlatantra

Verses 6.179–182 give postures for the six acts; see Part IV, section 3.4.

Merutantra

The postures for the six acts have bracketed definitions taken from Śāradātilaka (Muktabodha M00195, file lines 9207–9327; Part IV, section 3.4). The index also lists Meru under siddhāsana through a quotation from the Śivasaṃhitā; this is a quotation, not an independent Meru description.

Vārāhītantra

Chapter 26 contains a block of haṭha-yoga postures close to the Haṭhapradīpikā; pīṭha is used as a synonym for āsana. See Part IV, section 3.5.

Ācārasāratantra (Cīnācāra; manuscript M00501)

M00501 is a composite manuscript: file lines 1–1085 contain four chapters of the Haṭhapradīpikā, not the Ācārasāratantra. The Ācārasāra proper includes baddhāsana (line 2623); in the appended Samayāstotra, postures are distributed over the six acts (lines 4742–4744). See Part IV, section 3.5.

Mantrakaumudī

For japa the catalogue lists svastika,padmaka,vīra,vajra with definitions, including material from the Nāradīya; it separately names kukkuṭāsana and bhadrāsana and records a prohibition of utkaṭāsana during japa. See Part IV, section 3.6.

Muṇḍamālātantra (I)

Verses 4.48–50 list “twelve seats”; among the names are bhadrapīṭha,padma,siddha, siddhasiddha,devyāsana,kukkuṭa,vīra,varada,siṃha,śmaśāna and others. The catalogue explicitly notes that bodily postures and ritual seats are mixed (Part IV, section 3.6).

Muṇḍamālātantra (II)

The stanzas on bhadrāsana, kaurma, padmāsana and vīrāsana (Muktabodha M00538, file lines 479–487) coincide almost verbatim with Hāhārāvatantra M00283 (lines 11164–11171). This is a shared block, not an independent description of each posture. See Part IV, section 3.6.

Hāhārāvatantra

Hāhārāva M00283, file lines 11164–11171, almost verbatim matches the Muṇḍamālā II block M00538, lines 479–487, naming bhadrāsana, kaurma, padmāsana and vīrāsana. The entry also lists ūrdhvapāde sthito and baddhapadmāsana; a separate passage, padmāsanaṃ sādhayāmi … nāsāgre cintayen nityam, is assigned to Hāhārāva M00283, line 3768. See Part IV, section 3.6.

Kriyoḍḍīśamahātantrarāja

Passage 9.1 names siddhāsana and kamalāsana (Muktabodha M00512, file lines 391–392); the following passage concerns seat material, not another posture. Catalogue entry 50 also places 9.1 under Parameśvarīmata but cites this same Kriyoḍḍīśamahātantrarāja e-text; an independent Parameśvarīmata reading is not confirmed. See Part IV, section 3.6.

Parameśvarīmata

In M00218, utkaṭāsana is prohibited during japa (file line 7107), svastikāsana is named in connection with a mudrā (line 8441), and śūladaṇḍāsana is a mantra-extraction term, not a posture name. Separately, catalogue entry 50 gives 9.1 but cites Kriyoḍḍīśamahātantrarāja M00512, lines 391–392; the following passage concerns seat material, so an independent Parameśvarīmata reading of 9.1 is not confirmed. See Part IV, sections 3.6 and 8.

Kubjikātantra (the Bengali recension)

In prose, bhadrāsane copaviśya (“having sat on the bhadrāsana”) occurs; bhadrāsana is probably a seat in this context (Muktabodha M00030, file line 2382). See Part IV, section 3.6.

Kulānandādi

pūrvāmukha uttarāmukhaṃ vā padmāsanaṃ vadhvā: the instruction is to bind the padmāsana facing east or north (Muktabodha M00284, file line 451). See Part IV, section 3.6.

Śrīvidyārṇava(tantra)

This is a nibandha, a compilation of quotations with short connecting remarks. Posture references: siddhāsanastho in the description of the guru (Muktabodha M00217, file line 2902); baddhapadmāsano maunī (line 21852); baddhavīrāsana (line 34193); and japa prohibitions in a quotation from the Mantratantraprakāśa (line 31865). See Part IV, sections 3.6 and 6.

Śaktisaṅgamatantra, Tārākhaṇḍa

M00119 (file lines 1475–1611, 4107 and 4122) mentions vīrāsana, mahāvīrāsana and pañcavīrāsana. In a Śākta context, vīrāsana may denote the hero’s ritual seat, including on a corpse. See Part IV, section 3.6.

Mahānirvāṇatantra

Verse 3.51 includes baddhapadmāsanādīni; the commentary adds muktapadmāsana. See Part IV, section 3.6.

Kulamuktikallolinī

M00318 file lines 11025–11036 give vīrāsana with a quotation from the “Gorakṣasaṃhitā”; lines 30761–30789 list the ritual ṣaṭkarman postures: padma, svastika, vikaṭa, kukkuṭa, vajra and bhadra. See Part IV, section 3.6.

Īśānaśivagurudevapaddhati

Sāmānyapāda 5.64–78 (sādhanapaṭala) contains eleven postures with descriptions and purposes; karaṇa is mentioned in volume 3; the Yogapāda and 17.21 (iconography) are listed separately. See Part IV, section 4.1.

Kriyākramadyotikā (Aghoraśiva)

Baddhavīrāsana is given for eating, in a passage citing the Suprabheda. This is a ritual instruction for posture during a meal, not a separate description of a new posture. See Part IV, section 4.2.

Somaśambhupaddhati-vyākhyā (Trilocanaśiva)

Baddhavīrāsana is prescribed while eating, with a quotation from the Kiraṇa-tantra; autkuṭya (“squatting”) is among the prohibitions while eating. This is a ritual instruction for posture during a meal, not a separate āsana description. See Part IV, sections 1.10 and 4.2.

Śivārcanacandrikā (attributed to Appaya Dīkṣita)

Kukkuṭāsana is prescribed at nyāsa (Muktabodha M00079, file line 296). See Part IV, section 4.2.

Siddhāntasārapaddhati (Bhojadeva)

Karaṇa is cited as a parallel per Kafle; prāṇāyāma and sakalīkaraṇa are also mentioned in the daily rite. See Part IV, sections 4.2 and 11.

Pāśupatasūtra with Kauṇḍinya’s Pañcārthabhāṣya

For Pāśupatasūtra 1.16/17, Kauṇḍinya gives seven postures: padmaka, svastika, upasthāñjalika, ardhacandra, pīṭhaka, daṇḍāyata and sarvatobhadra (psbh_1.16:10). Kafle counts eight; Mallinson and Singleton count seven. This is a parallel outside the scope of tantras and āgamas. See Part IV, sections 7, 11 and 14.
